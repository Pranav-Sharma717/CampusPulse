AI Executive Synthesis

Database normalization is the systematic process of organizing fields and tables within a relational database to minimize redundancy and eliminate anomalies (insertion, update, and deletion anomalies). The syllabus unit focuses on functional dependencies (FDs), canonical cover computations, and progressive normal forms from 1NF to Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF).

A critical exam distinction highlighted in these slides: 3NF preserves functional dependencies and is always lossless, whereas BCNF guarantees complete elimination of redundancy based on functional dependencies, but may not always preserve all original FDs upon decomposition.

💡 High-Probability Exam Alert: Professor emphasized Question 2 on the Midterm will test whether a candidate relation R(A, B, C, D) with F = {A → B, B → C, C → D} can be decomposed into BCNF without losing dependencies.

Core Theorems & High-Yield Key Points:

  • Armstrong's Axioms: Reflexivity (Y ⊆ X ⇒ X → Y), Augmentation (X → Y ⇒ XZ → YZ), and Transitivity (X → Y, Y → Z ⇒ X → Z).
  • Lossless-Join Decomposition Condition: A decomposition of R into R1 and R2 is lossless if and only if (R1 ∩ R2) → R1 OR (R1 ∩ R2) → R2.
  • BCNF Criterion: For every non-trivial functional dependency X → Y, the left side X MUST be a superkey for the relation.
  • 3NF Criterion: For every non-trivial X → Y, either X is a superkey OR Y is a prime attribute (part of some candidate key).

Predicted 5-Mark & 10-Mark University Questions:

Q1 (10 Marks): Compare 3NF and BCNF with a suitable real-world schema.

Expected answer key: Define both forms; demonstrate a relation where 3NF is achieved but BCNF fails without sacrificing dependency preservation.

Q2 (5 Marks): Prove that any relation with two attributes is always in BCNF.

Expected answer key: Analyze non-trivial FDs possible for R(A, B). In all non-trivial cases, the LHS forms a candidate key.

1. LLM for Summarization & Planning

Lecture slide decks and syllabus PDFs are parsed into semantic chunks, vectorized, and queried through localized Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to generate exam summaries, flashcards, and spaced study plans without hallucination.

2. Hybrid Recommendation System

Unifies multiple machine learning strategies to deliver highly accurate opportunity and resource suggestions. Solves the cold-start problem by falling back to verified syllabus prerequisites.

3. Content-Based Filtering

Extracts feature vectors from course enrollments (e.g. SQL, C++, POSIX threads) and matches them against internship role descriptions using Cosine Similarity over term embeddings.

4. Collaborative Filtering

Analyzes historical engagement and placement success patterns of senior students in the same department to surface high-converting hackathons and second-hand semester textbooks.

5. Rule-Based Priority Scoring

Enforces hard constraints: Deadlines under 24 hours, mandatory lab prerequisites, and zero-balance budget safety floors override probabilistic recommendations immediately.

6. Context-Aware Ranking (CAR)

Dynamically recalculates candidate rank weights based on temporal proximity to exams (Δt), current available weekly budget, time of day, and student fatigue index.
